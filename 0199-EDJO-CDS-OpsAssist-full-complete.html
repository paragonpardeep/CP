01Where we started

The pain we started from

Before OpsAssist, support engineers spent significant time manually searching across Jira, Confluence, GitHub, ServiceNow, and knowledge repositories to gather incident context. Context switching, manual root-cause analysis, dependence on tribal knowledge, and scattered information slowed investigations, delayed onboarding, and created inconsistent support outcomes for engineers, engineering managers, and client stakeholders.

02North star

Our North Star is to build a Support Engineering AI Factory where routine support and investigation activities become AI-assisted while governance and business-critical decisions remain human-controlled. OpsAssist will support incident triage, knowledge discovery, RCA recommendations, cross-system correlation, documentation generation, delivery analytics, and ticket preparation. Human engineers will retain final approvals, production decisions, risk assessment, change-management approvals, customer communication, escalation, and exception handling.

03How it is built

A user request flows through the Elitea-based OpsAssist architecture: User Request → Intent Router → Skill Selection → Toolkit Execution → Data Correlation → AI Analysis → Human Review → Approved Action. The solution uses governed skills for cross-system correlation, RCA, developer workflows, analytics, and controlled write operations. Jira, Confluence, GitHub, and ServiceNow toolkits provide enterprise data access, and human approval is required before any write operation.

10What we learned

  1. Enterprise integration drives AI value

    OpsAssist delivers the most value when it can retrieve and correlate evidence across operational systems rather than operating as a standalone assistant.

  2. Governance and human oversight remain critical

    Human review, approval, risk assessment, and production decision-making are necessary for safe use, particularly for controlled write actions.

  3. Access and approvals can be the main delivery constraint

    Data-source connectivity, security review, development-environment access, and platform approvals may become bigger blockers than the AI implementation itself.

  4. Reuse accelerates delivery

    Approved reusable assets, frameworks, and agents reduce implementation effort when they are adapted to project-specific workflows and governance requirements.

11What is stuck

  • ServiceNow integration approval and development-instance access remain the primary blockers to broader incident intelligence, historical pattern analysis, and automated operational workflows.

    Secure ServiceNow approvals, development-environment access, and the required dependency resolution with platform and administration teams.

    Needs: Support from platform teams, ServiceNow administrators, and account leadership to complete approvals and environment access.

12If you are building the same factory

Start with a focused, high-value support use case, reuse approved assets, define human decision points, establish strong governance, and design measurable business outcomes from the beginning. Start enterprise access and approval activities early, and expand only after the workflow is reliable and evidence-backed.

Would you call this an AI Factory?

Not fully yet, but it is evolving toward one. OpsAssist is currently a governed AI-enabled support platform. With broader ServiceNow integration, measurable outcomes, reusable marketplace publication, expanded automation, and multi-team adoption, it will represent a mature Support Engineering AI Factory.

How is the programme working for you?

The clear framework, AI coaching support, reuse mindset, and structured maturity journey are working well. Earlier clarification of AI Factory definitions, faster integration approvals, and additional guidance on measurement expectations would improve execution.
